# 🌀 Word of Bewilderment

## “The Prince of Fools”

### Core Theme

Identity, confusion, perception — and the cost of bargains.

This quest should feel mirthful, unfair, and deeply surreal. The Feywild does not punish cruelty so much as it rewards spectacle.

---

# 📖 What Has Happened (Session Recap)

1. Party researched Words of Power at **Candlekeep**.
2. **Helmeuth** learned a powerful god or archfey of revelry could bestow the Word of Bewilderment.
3. He chose **Hyrsam, the Prince of Fools**.
4. Party traveled the **Celestial Stairway** to the **Nowhere Inn**.
5. They found a **Dryad of the Summer Court** and asked her to take them to Hyrsam.
6. She agreed to guide them — if they offered something in return.
7. Before anyone else spoke, **Hugs** said *"Here you can have Helmeuth,"* grappled him, and handed him over.
8. The Feywild enforced the bargain. **Helmeuth vanished.** Session ended.

**Hyrsam is currently at the Summer Court**, hosting entertainment for **Titania**. The Dryad can still guide the party there — the contract is paid.

---

# 🧠 The Truth Behind Helmeuth's Disappearance

The Dryad owed a **debt** to a powerful Summer Court noble:

### Lady Meliantha, Duchess of Lasting Blooms

A vain, glittering archfey of the Summer Court who collects *amusing mortals* the way others collect jewels. The Dryad's debt was overdue. When Helmeuth was offered, the Feywild did not leave him with the Dryad — it **passed him up the chain** to settle what was owed.

Meliantha, delighted with her new curiosity, **gifted Helmeuth to Hyrsam** as the evening's fool for Titania's revel.

When the party arrives, they find:

> Helmeuth already on the stage — painted, belled, and dressed as the Fool — juggling jokes, humiliations, and half-truths for a roaring Summer Court crowd. He recognizes the party. He cannot simply leave.

# 🏙️ Quest Structure (From Here)

## Phase 1 — Arrival at the Revel

The Dryad leads them to the edge of Titania's midsummer entertainment: lantern-lit groves, floating tables, rivers of wine, music that rearranges your memories of the last song.

### Immediate Hooks

* Spot Helmeuth performing (Insight / Perception).
* Learn Meliantha "owns" his presence for the night (gather info from revelers).
* Hear that Hyrsam will grant a boon to whoever most **dazzles the Court** before dawn.

### Atmosphere Notes

* Names slip. Guests introduce themselves differently each conversation.
* Clocks lie. Time stretches and compresses around performances.
* Refusing a drink is ruder than starting a duel.
* Mortals who take themselves too seriously become the entertainment.

### Laws of the Revel (Titania's Decree)

Before the party is fully admitted, a herald (or the Dryad) recites the Queen's laws. These are **literal**. The Court adores loopholes; it does not forgive ignorance.

#### I. There Shall Be No Spilling of Blood

Guests are welcome to dispute. They are even welcome to come to blows.

But should a **single drop of blood** be spilled, **the Summer Knight** — Lady Titania's most prized champion — will seek the **immediate death** of whoever broke the rule.

**DM notes:**

* Bruises, broken pride, wrestling, magical non-lethal force, sleep, charm, and humiliation are all fair game.
* Piercing, slashing, and most necrotic/"wound" effects that draw blood are not.
* If *both* parties bleed in the same clash, the Knight may hunt **both** — or ask Titania which fool was more entertaining first.
* Self-inflicted blood (biting a lip, a cut palm for a vow) still counts unless Titania laughs it off.
* Fey and guests police this gleefully: someone may *try* to make a PC bleed so the Knight falls on them.

#### II. No Guest Shall Refuse the Queen's Toast

When Titania raises a cup, all drink. Refusal is treason of manners.

* The toast is usually harmless revel-wine.
* Sometimes it is a mild charm, truth-tingle, or memory-blur (Con or Wis save DC 14 to shrug the side effect; you still must drink).

#### III. A Challenge Accepted Must Be Seen Through

Walk away from a duel of wit, strength, or spectacle after accepting, and you forfeit more than the match — Meliantha or Hyrsam may claim a favor, an item, or a temporary service.

* Declining *before* accepting is allowed (and sometimes wise).
* Cheating is encouraged if it makes a better story.

#### IV. Do Not Mar the Midsummer With Cold Iron

Cold iron weapons and bindings are to be sheathed, covered, or left at the grove's edge.

* Brandishing cold iron is a scandal; drawing blood with it is Rule I *and* an insult to Summer.

#### V. The Revel Ends at Dawn — Leave With What You Are Owed

When the first true sunbeam touches the Queen's pavilion, contests stop. Claims unresolved become Meliantha's gossip and Hyrsam's unfinished jokes.

* "What you are owed" includes bargains struck *during* the night — another reason to settle Helmeuth before dawn.

---

### The Summer Knight (Enforcer)

Titania's champion. Appearances matter: gleaming armor of living leaf-gold, a blade that never needs to cut to kill, a calm that makes Hyrsam look drunk.

**Role at the table:**

* Visible early — a reminder, not a random boss fight.
* Intervenes **instantly** if Rule I is broken. No debate, no initiative for "talking them down" unless Titania herself stays the Knight's hand.
* Does not police bruises, insults, theft of applause, or humiliating Helmeuth. Those are sport.
* If the party somehow defeats or flees the Knight after spilling blood, they have declared war on Summer's hospitality — the revel turns, and escape becomes the new adventure.

**Combat note (only if Rule I breaks):** treat as a deadly solo threat (legendary actions / lair-like revel terrain). Prefer a chase or a single overwhelming strike over a long slog — the point is consequence, not a fair duel.

## Phase 2 — Two Intertwined Goals

| Goal | Who controls it | What they want |
|------|-----------------|----------------|
| **Recover Helmeuth** | Lady Meliantha (debt-holder) / Hyrsam (current "user") | Amusement, status, a replacement spectacle |
| **Acquire the Word** | Hyrsam | A party so good Titania remembers it for a century |

These can be solved together or separately. Clever parties will braid them.

---

# 1. How Does the Party Get Helmeuth Back?

Present **multiple valid paths**. Let the table invent more. Fey contracts hate being boring more than they hate being broken — but breaking them still has teeth.

### Path A — Buy Him Out (Negotiation)

Meliantha will release her claim if offered something of **equal or greater entertainment value**.

Examples she might accept:

* A true secret that would embarrass a god or archfey
* A performance so good she forgets she ever wanted a pet fool
* Another willing "curiosity" (party should feel the moral weight)
* A favor owed by the party (dangerous — write the wording carefully)
* Helmeuth's own *name* spoken in a way that makes him *interesting* rather than *owned* (roleplay + Persuasion/Performance)

**DC guidance:** start around 15–18 Cha checks; lower if the offer is genuinely spectacular, raise if they treat her like a merchant.

### Path B — Win Him (Wager)

Challenge Meliantha or Hyrsam to a contest with Helmeuth as the stake.

* Hyrsam loves this.
* Meliantha only accepts if losing would still look glamorous.
* If the party loses, Meliantha may claim a *second* party member for the evening.

### Path C — Steal the Fool (Heist)

Possible, but loud — and it must obey **Rule I** (no blood).

Complications:

* Helmeuth's fool-costume is a **minor geas** — leaving the revel grounds without permission triggers bells heard by every Summer Court sentinel.
* Meliantha's roses animate as pursuers (thorns that *bruise and bind*, not bleed — unless someone is careless).
* Hyrsam may *applaud* a clever theft… then demand they stay and finish the show anyway.
* If a guard is cut and bleeds, the heist ends and the **Summer Knight** begins.

### Path D — Free Him Through Spectacle

If Helmeuth (with or without the party) becomes the night's undeniable star, Hyrsam may declare:

> "A fool who owns the room cannot be owned by a lady."

This frees Helmeuth *and* advances the Word goal. Best "clean" ending if the table leans heroic/comedic.

### Path E — Helmeuth Chooses the Revel

Optional dark beat: Helmeuth is good at this. The Court loves him. Offer him a chance to stay as Hyrsam's fool permanently in exchange for the Word whispered into his ear.

If he accepts, he gains the Word — and leaves the party (temporarily or permanently). Heavy character moment; only spring if it fits the table.

### Contract Reminder (DM)

Hugs's bargain with the Dryad is **already complete**. The party does not need to "undo" that deal to free Helmeuth. They must deal with **Meliantha's claim** (and Hyrsam's use of him). The Dryad may even help — she got what she needed — but she will not fight Meliantha for free.

# 2. How Does Helmeuth Acquire the Word?

## Hyrsam's Desire

Hyrsam wants **one thing**: a revel so spectacular that Titania is genuinely surprised.

He will teach / bestow the **Word of Bewilderment** to the mortal (or mortals) who most contribute to that spectacle before dawn.

### Framing for Players

Hyrsam, grinning too wide:

> "Confusion is the highest art. Make them doubt the floor, the sky, and their own punchlines — and I will teach you the oldest joke in creation."

The Word is not a scroll to steal. It is a *performance secret* Hyrsam speaks into a worthy fool's mind.

---

## Challenge Design: The Midsummer Contests

Use a **flexible contest structure**, not a single railroaded puzzle.

### Recommended Structure — "Three Triumphs Before Dawn"

The party (and/or Helmeuth) must earn **3 Triumphs** before the revel ends.

Each Triumph comes from succeeding at a contest, staging an unrehearsed spectacle, or creatively breaking a contest in a way that delights Hyrsam.

**Failure is not death** — failure is *becoming the joke*. Embarrassing complications, temporary charms, cosmetic curses, lost items that reappear as party favors, etc.

---

### Contests of Mirth (Pick 4–6; let players choose / invent)

#### 1. The River of Mead
Drink, divert, or otherwise "empty" a stream of enchanted mead.

* **Straight:** series of Con saves (DC 14, 16, 18). Failures = levels of exhaustion or *confusion*.
* **Clever:** Evocation to freeze/boil it, Performance to convince the river it has already been drunk, Athletics to wrestle the river-spirit, etc.
* **Triumph if:** the Court cheers the method, not just the result.

#### 2. Staring Contest with a Beholder
A beholder (or beholder-ish fey monstrosity) named **Gaze-for-Days**.

* **Straight:** contested Wis checks; on a loss, hit with a random mild eye-ray (charm, slow, fear — comedy versions).
* **Clever:** mirrors, illusions, getting it to look at itself, closing *its* eyes with a lullaby.
* **Bewilderment beat:** the longer you stare, the less sure you are which of you is the beholder.

#### 3. Arm Wrestling the Hill-Giant King
**King Grumblethane**, already deep in his cups.

* **Straight:** Athletics contests (Advantage if drunk on fey wine — but Wis saves to stay focused).
* **Clever:** grease, enlarge/reduce, persuading him the match is already over, challenging his *pride* instead of his arm.
* If they win too easily, he demands best of three — and the crowd loves it.

#### 4. Make the Queen Laugh
Optional **finale-tier** contest. Titania has seen everything.

* Requires a genuine creative performance from the players (not just a roll).
* Roll Performance / Persuasion / Deception only *after* they describe the bit.
* Success = automatic Triumph + Hyrsam's favor.
* Partial success = polite royal smile (no Triumph, but Meliantha notices).

#### 5. The Duel of Stories
Trade tales with a sidhe bard. Each round, the story must be more confusing than the last without losing the audience.

* Skill challenge (5 successes before 3 failures): Performance, Deception, Arcana, History, Insight.
* **Word foreshadowing:** the winning story briefly makes listeners forget their own names — then returns them, laughing.

#### 6. Dance Until the Moon Blinks
Stay in the revel-dance until a fey moon "blinks" (variable time).

* Con / Performance / Acrobatics rotation.
* Partners change every minute — including enemies, statues, and your own shadow.
* Failure = dancing for 1d4 hours after the party (exhaustion, comedy chase).

#### 7. Steal Applause from Hyrsam Himself
During Helmeuth's fool-act, upstage Hyrsam *without* insulting him.

* Hard (DC 18+), but Hyrsam respects thieves of attention.
* Success bonds Helmeuth's freedom path (Path D) to the Word path.

#### 8. Player-Invented Spectacle (Always Allowed)
If the party proposes something funnier / stranger than your list, **that is the correct contest**. Assign an ability check or short skill challenge and lean into it.

---

### Tracking Triumphs

| Triumphs | Hyrsam's Reaction |
|----------|-------------------|
| 0 | Polite interest; Helmeuth remains Meliantha's toy |
| 1 | "Promising." Offers a hint about Meliantha's desires |
| 2 | "Almost worthy." May loan Helmeuth to the party *for one contest* |
| 3 | Bestows the Word; will advocate (or demand) Helmeuth's release if asked as part of the encore |

---

## Bestowing the Word

When the third Triumph lands (or Titania laughs), Hyrsam draws the winner close and whispers something that sounds like a punchline and a scream at once.

### Who receives it?

Default: **Helmeuth**, since this is his Word quest — especially if he participated.

Alternatives:

* Split: Helmeuth receives the Word; the party receives a boon (safe passage from the Court, Meliantha's debt cleared, a favor-token).
* If Helmeuth is still enslaved and absent from the triumphs, Hyrsam may give the Word to a party member instead — creating tension about whether they return it to Helmeuth.

Mechanical effects: see `Word of Bewilderment Description.md` (Base Form → attunements). First receipt = **Base Form**. The revel itself can count toward **Initial Attunement** if someone unravels a comforting lie at Court, or **Full Attunement** foreshadowing if Helmeuth loses and rebuilds his dignity after being the Fool.

# 🎭 Key NPCs

### Hyrsam, Prince of Fools
Archfey of revelry, satire, and sacred mockery. Friendly the way a wildfire is warm. Judges spectacle over morality. Will absolutely gift cosmic power for a good show.

### Lady Meliantha, Duchess of Lasting Blooms
Summer Court noble. Owns the debt that claimed Helmeuth. Wants beauty, gossip, and to be seen owning interesting things. Not evil — possessive. Can be reasoned with if the party makes her look magnificent.

### The Dryad Guide
Still present. Debt cleared. Helpful tour guide / unreliable narrator. Will not openly oppose Meliantha. Might tip the party off to Path A or D if treated kindly.

### Helmeuth, the Reluctant Fool
Play him aware and angry *or* unexpectedly thriving — either works. Give him agency: whispered asides to the party, sabotage of Meliantha's dignity, or a plea to not "rescue" him in a way that makes the Court hunt them forever.

### The Summer Knight
Titania's enforcer of the revel laws — especially **no spilled blood**. Silent, magnificent, final. A living sword of hospitality. See Laws of the Revel above.

# 🌀 Exit & Aftermath

Once Helmeuth is free (or chooses to stay) and the Word is bestowed (or refused):

* Dawn breaks wrong — the revel folds like a carnival tent.
* The Dryad offers a path back toward the Nowhere Inn / Celestial Stairway.
* Meliantha may remain an ally, gossip contact, or future creditor depending on how she was treated.
* Hyrsam tip of the hat: he may appear later when the party takes themselves too seriously.

### If they fail before dawn

They are not killed. They wake in a dew-soaked grove outside the Court with:

* hangover levels of exhaustion,
* flower crowns they don't remember accepting,
* Helmeuth still missing (or returned with no memory of the Word),
* and an invitation etched on birch bark: *"Try again next revel. Bring better material."*

# 📝 DM Quick Reference

1. **Dryad bargain is done** — Helmeuth went to Meliantha, then to Hyrsam's stage.
2. **Titania's laws bind the revel** — fights OK; **one drop of blood** = Summer Knight seeks death.
3. **Free Helmeuth** via buyout, wager, bloodless heist, spectacle, or his choice to stay.
4. **Earn the Word** via ~3 Triumphs of mirth before dawn; open-ended performances count.
5. **Braid the goals** when possible — Path D + three Triumphs is the cleanest dual win.
6. Reward creativity harder than optimal dice.
